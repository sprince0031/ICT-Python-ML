8. Accuracy just tells us the total percentage of correct guesses. But think about our data: it's 90% 'Healthy'. A lazy model could just guess 'Healthy' every single time and it would be 90% accurate right out of the box! Is our 96% model any better than that? We need to dig deeper.

To really understand our model's performance, we need to look at the Confusion Matrix.

A confusion matrix is a table that shows us where our model made mistakes. It breaks down all our predictions into four categories. Let's generate one for our model using seaborn.
------------------------------------------------
9.
start by initializing the confusion matrix 
cm = confusion_matrix(y_c_test, y_c_pred)

then we set the figure size 

we are plotting it using sns.heatmap. Lets run it now

Okay, let's read this. The Y-axis is the 'Actual' truth, and the X-axis is what our model 'Predicted'.

True negatives are in the top left. We predicted 'Healthy' 357 times, and the patient was Healthy. That's great.

In the bottom right, we have true positives. We predicted 'Sick' 26 times, and the patient was Sick. Also great.
-------------------------------------------------
10. Now see the top right for false positives. We predicted 'Sick' 10 times, but the patient was Healthy. This is an error. We might cause a patient to panic or undergo unnecessary tests.

Check the bottom left now, these are the false negatives. We predicted 'Healthy' 7 times, but the patient was Sick. This is the most dangerous error. We just told 7 sick people that they are perfectly healthy and sent them home.

This matrix gives us so much more context than a simple accuracy score. These four numbers are what we use to calculate more advanced metrics.

Which brings us to the Classification Report.
----------------------------------------------------
11. This report is the true story of our model. Let's break it down, focusing on our minority class, 'Sick'. This is the class we care most about.

Look at the 'Sick' row. The precision is 0.72. What does this mean? The formula is True Positives / (True Positives + False Positives). In our case, 26 / (26 + 10) = 0.72
Precision asks: Of all the times our model predicted 'Sick', what percentage was correct? Our model was correct 72% of the time. The other 28% were False Positives.
---------------------------------------------------
12. Moving on to recall, which is more important for us. The recall for 'Sick' is 0.79. The formula is True Positives / (True Positives + False Negatives). In our case, 26 / (26 + 7) = 0.79. Recall asks: Of all the patients who were actually sick, what percentage did our model find? Our model only found 79% of them! It missed 21% of all sick patients: those 7 False Negatives. This is a failing model, and the 96% accuracy score completely hid this fact!

Now, we have the F1 score. This is just the harmonic mean of Precision and Recall. It's a single, balanced metric that's useful when you care about both False Positives and False Negatives. At 0.75, it's a much more honest reflection of our model's performance than the 96% accuracy.
